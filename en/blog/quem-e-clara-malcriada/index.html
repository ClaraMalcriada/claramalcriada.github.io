When Irony Becomes Art: Who Is Clara Malcriada

February 3, 2027 · 3 min read · by Clara Malcriada

The name explains the project. "Clara" is what one expects of a writer: clarity, method, bibliography. "Malcriada" — Portuguese for badly raised, unmannered — is what the bibliography produces. Between the two words lies an entire course of reading: someone who studied the great rhetorical traditions of the world in earnest and concluded, to the enormous disappointment of the curriculum, that humanity perfected the insult before perfecting nearly anything else.

The project

Clara Malcriada is a Brazilian author of sarcastic books on insults, irony and elegant verbal comebacks. Her work starts from an uncomfortable thesis: every civilization developed, before the internet, a complete system of refined discontent — and what the internet did was dump that patrimony onto a platform where nobody has more repertoire than urgency.

The Art of Insults recovers these systems one by one. About Clara Malcriada holds the full inventory; the summary is as follows:

The Art of the Chinese Indirection documents the school of subtext: two thousand years of proverbs, letters and court anecdotes teaching the offense whose assembly is the target's job. Maximum compression, zero drama, patrimony certified by dynasties.

Russian Insults covers the school of density: a country where complaining out loud cost you dearly for centuries produced insults that carry irony, history and prognosis in one sentence. Tsars, revolutions and memes evaluated the material; it passed every exam.

Arabic Insults organizes the school of rhetoric: parlor curses, blessings that work like plagues and proverbs of grammatical disqualification — the tradition that discovered the courteous wish offends more than the open plague.

The Art of the Persian Insult closes the series with the school of metaphor: the Iranian tradition criticizes the way Hafez wrote, with flowers and a thorn in the center. It is the most beautiful insult in the world repertoire — the kind the victim frames before realizing they were the target.

The method

Each volume follows the protocol of an academic research project without the academy's authorization: historical sources, context, commented examples and guides for contemporary application. The books do not teach shouting — they teach building. The editorial premise is that verbal aggression is the symptom of an undersized vocabulary, and that whoever mastered the repertoire never needed volume.

The reader who arrives at this blog through a search engine is generally after one of three things: an indirection for the family group chat, an answer to a ten-minute voice note, or an explanation of why they are still thinking about what they should have said in the meeting. The posts try to solve the immediate; the books solve the structural.

Irony is called art when it outlives its author. These traditions outlived their authors, their censors and their dynasties — and now have, for the first time in English as well as Portuguese, an organized catalog. Be it said in defense of civilization: it always knew how to insult well. What was missing was someone to catalog it. And someone willing to sign the catalog with a name that admits the trade out loud: clear in method, unmannered in intent.

Who is Clara Malcriada?

Which book of The Art of Insults should I read first?

Russian Insults works well as an entry point for new readers; The Art of the Chinese Indirection is the shortest and introduces the series' method. The next two, Arabic Insults and The Art of the Persian Insult, deepen rhetoric and metaphor.
